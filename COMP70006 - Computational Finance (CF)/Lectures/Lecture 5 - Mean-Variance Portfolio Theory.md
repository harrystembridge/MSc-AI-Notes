> [!CARD] What condition do the asset weights $w_i$ of a portfolio satisfy?
> They sum to 1: $\sum_{i=1}^n w_i = 1$.

> [!CARD] In portfolio theory, what does $R_i$ denote?
> The total return of asset $i$ (end value per unit invested, $R_i = 1 + r_i$).

> [!CARD] What amount of money does the $i$th asset generate at the end of the period?
> $R_i X_{0i} = R_i w_i X_0$, where $X_0$ is the initial budget and $w_i$ is the weight of asset $i$.

> [!CARD] What is the total value of the portfolio after the period?
> $\sum_{i=1}^n R_i w_i X_0$

> [!CARD] What is the portfolio's total return $R$ in terms of the asset weights and returns?
> $R = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i R_i X_0}{X_0} = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i R_i$

> [!CARD] What is the portfolio's rate of return $r$ in terms of the asset weights and rates of return?
> $r = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i r_i$, a weighted average of the individual rates of return.

- Assume that an investor is risk averse.
- Given two portfolios with the exact same return, the investor will favour that with the lowest risk.
- Investor is also greedy, so given same risk, investor will choose the one with the highest return.
