> [!CARD] What is a financial instrument?
> A legal obligation or claim having monetary value (e.g. stocks, bonds, mortgages, futures, insurance).

> [!CARD] What is a security?
> A tradable financial instrument satisfying legal and regulatory requirements.

> [!CARD] What is a fixed-income security?
> A security that promises a fixed (definite) income over a span of time, e.g. bonds, mortgages, annuities.

> [!CARD] What does a fixed-income security represent in terms of cash flows?
> The ownership of a definite cash flow stream.


![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=4|CF - Lecture 4, page 4]]

> [!CARD] What risk does the issuer of a fixed-income security pose?
> The issuer could default (e.g. go bankrupt), so there is a typically small chance the promised income is discontinued or delayed.

> [!CARD] Which securities with contingent cash flows are still called fixed-income?
> Adjustable-rate mortgages and callable bonds.

> [!CARD] What is the general idea of a fixed-income security's cash flow stream?
> It is fixed except in well-defined contingent circumstances.

![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=5|CF - Lecture 4, page 5]]

> [!CARD] What is an annuity?
> A contract that pays the holder money periodically according to a fixed schedule (e.g. pension benefits).

> [!CARD] What is the present value of an annuity paying a fixed amount $A$ at the end of each of $n$ equally spaced periods?
> $PV = \sum_{k=1}^n d_k A$, where $d_k$ is the discount factor for period $k$.


![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=6|CF - Lecture 4, page 6]]

> [!CARD] What is the finite geometric series $S_n = \sum_{k=0}^n x^k$ in closed form?
> $S_n = \dfrac{1 - x^{n+1}}{1 - x}$.

> [!CARD] Which two recursions are combined to derive the geometric series formula?
> $S_{n+1} = 1 + x S_n$ and $S_{n+1} = S_n + x^{n+1}$.

> [!CARD] What is the infinite geometric series $S_\infty = \sum_{k=0}^\infty x^k$, and when does it hold?
> $S_\infty = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}$, for $|x| < 1$.


![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=7|CF - Lecture 4, page 7]]

> [!CARD] What is the present value of an annuity paying $A$ for $n$ periods if the spot rate curve is constant at $r$?
> $PV = \dfrac{A}{r}\left(1 - \dfrac{1}{(1+r)^n}\right)$.

> [!CARD] How do you solve for the annuity payment $A$ given $PV$, $r$ and $n$?
> $A = \dfrac{r(1+r)^n \, PV}{(1+r)^n - 1}$.

> [!CARD] What does a constant spot rate curve mean for an annuity?
> $s_k = r$ for all $k = 1, \dots, n$, so each payment is discounted as $A/(1+r)^k$.


![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=8|CF - Lecture 4, page 8]]

> [!CARD] What is amortization?
> Replacing a current obligation by periodic payments.

> [!CARD] A \$1,000 loan at 12% interest compounded monthly is repaid with equal monthly payments over 5 years. What are $r$ and $n$?
> $r = 12\%/12 = 1\%$ per month and $n = 5 \times 12 = 60$.

> [!CARD] What is the monthly payment on a \$1,000 loan at 12% compounded monthly over 5 years?
> $A = \dfrac{r(1+r)^n PV}{(1+r)^n - 1} = \$22.24$ per month.


![[CF - Lecture 4.pdf#page=9|CF - Lecture 4, page 9]]

> [!CARD] What is a bond?
> An agreement to pay money according to the rules of the issue.

> [!CARD] What is the maturity date of a bond?
> The time of the last payment.

> [!CARD] What is the face value (par value) of a bond?
> The amount to be paid at the maturity date.

> [!CARD] What is a coupon payment?
> An amount paid periodically, expressed as a percentage of the face value.

> [!CARD] When is the last coupon of a bond paid?
> At maturity.

> [!CARD] In bond notation, what do $F$ and $r_C$ stand for?
> $F$ is the face value and $r_C$ is the coupon rate.

> [!CARD] How do you calculate the yearly coupon amount $C$ of a bond?
> $C = r_C F$, the coupon rate times the face value.

> [!CARD] If a bond makes $m$ coupon payments per year, how large is each subannual coupon payment?
> $C/m$, which is $r_C F / m$.
